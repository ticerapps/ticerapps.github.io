Try it / chording

One tap. Every safe neighbour.

Chording opens the unflagged tiles around a number when its adjacent flags match that number. Prove the mines first: matching the count does not make a misplaced flag safe.

Read this 1–2–1 edge. In this trainer, tap a hidden tile to place or remove a flag, then tap the outlined 2 to chord. No timer, no saved score.

Explain the solution

The two outer tiles must be mines; the middle is safe. Flag the left and right tiles, then tap the 2. It opens the middle tile, which also reads 2 because it touches both mines. In the full game, hold or right-click to flag; tap a satisfied number to chord.

Worked example / reduction

A 2–3–2 hiding a 1–2–1.

A known flag does some of the counting for you. Subtract it from every clue it touches, including diagonally, to find how many mines are still unaccounted for.

  1. Start with a proven flag. The bottom flag is already known to be a mine. It touches all three clues.
  2. Subtract one from each clue. 2 − 1 = 1, 3 − 1 = 2, 2 − 1 = 1. You now need 1–2–1 mines in the hidden row.
  3. Read the remaining pattern. Each outer 1 allows only one mine in its pair. To satisfy the middle 2 as well, the two outer tiles must be mines.
  4. Flag the outside. Open the middle. The centre tile is safe. The real board numbers stay 2–3–2; the smaller numbers are your mental calculation.

Chord once the flags are proven.

When the adjacent flags match a number, tap that number to open its remaining neighbours. Chording trusts your flags; it does not check whether they are correct.

Mind the edges of odd shapes.

Boards that are not rectangles, like the coastline boards in Minesweeper: Strait of Hormuz, use the same eight-neighbour rule, diagonals included. Land removes neighbours, so count the actual tiles before applying an edge pattern.

A zero counter is not proof.

The mine counter subtracts placed flags, including wrong ones. Only treat the remaining tiles as safe when every flag is justified and every mine is accounted for.
